# Proper — What It Means For Me
### Money, Time, and Room to Create

*My own analysis — not the pitch I hand Proper. One question: if Proper acquires Smalley and retains me, what do I get?*

---

## The Structure in One Line

**I sell Smalley to Proper and they hire me to run it.** The coffees keep their names and stories but become Proper coffees. I get paid for the business *and* draw a salary to run the brand + build Proper's e-commerce. I stop owning it — and stop operating it almost entirely.

---

## The Money — Two Separate Checks

**Check #1: The acquisition (getting paid for the business)**
- **Lump sum:** ~$45,000–55,000 at closing, done, or
- **Hybrid (recommended):** ~$20,000 upfront + 5% royalty for 4 years (~$36K+ total, more if it grows), or
- **Royalty only:** 8% for 5 years, no cash upfront

**Check #2: The role (getting paid for my work)**
- Base salary/retainer (a floor I can count on)
- Commission on new wholesale accounts I open
- A share of e-commerce/subscription growth I drive
- Annual bonus on targets

**Why two checks matter:** the business has value whether or not I work there — that's Check #1. My labor is worth more on top — that's Check #2. I must never let Proper roll them into one "we'll pay you a bit more" number.

**The honest trade-off:** I cash out (real money now) but I give up the *upside*. If Smalley/the line triples under Proper, that growth is theirs, not mine — I only capture my royalty window and my salary. I'm trading equity for cash + security + freedom.

---

## The Time

**This is the maximum-freedom option.** Nearly everything operational leaves my plate:

- All packaging (~114 five-pound bags + ~200 smaller bags/month) — gone
- All fulfillment, shipping, inventory, green ordering — gone
- Production, roast scheduling, quality — Proper's problem now
- Even billing and back-office can shift to Proper

**What I'd actually spend time on:** the brand, the story, the subscription experience, and **building Proper's whole e-commerce channel** — high-leverage creative and strategic work, on someone else's payroll, with someone else handling every physical task.

Of the two paths, **this frees the most hours.** I go from owner-operator to a focused brand/e-commerce lead.

---

## Room to Create

I get to create — but for a brand I no longer own. The names and stories live on under Proper. That's the emotional cost: my creative work builds *their* asset now. Upside: I create with real resources behind me (an established roaster, a real company) instead of doing it solo and broke on time.

---

## What I Give Up

- **Ownership and all future upside** — the big one
- **The brand as *mine*** — Smalley the name retires; my coffees become Proper's
- **Independence** — I'm an employee, subject to their direction and their continued existence

---

## Bottom Line

**Proper = sell the baby, get paid, go nearly chore-free.** I collect cash now (~$36–55K depending on structure), draw a salary, and offload essentially 100% of operations. Best path if I want money in hand, maximum time freedom, and I'm at peace letting the brand become part of something bigger.

The catch is permanent: I stop owning what I built.
