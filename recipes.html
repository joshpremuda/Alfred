Recipe Guide

Ideas for your menu — built for wholesale partners. Every recipe is a starting point; adjust ratios and featured coffee to fit your program.

Sparkling & Tonic

Grapefruit Espresso Tonic

Sparkling
  • Double espresso
  • Tonic water
  • Fresh grapefruit juice or zest
  • Ice

Pull espresso over ice. Top with tonic. Finish with a squeeze of grapefruit and an expressed peel.

Orange Marmalade Espresso Tonic

Sparkling
  • Double espresso
  • 1 tsp orange marmalade
  • Tonic water
  • Ice

Stir marmalade into the bottom of the glass. Add ice. Pull espresso directly over, then top with tonic. The marmalade melts as you stir.

AeroPress Tonic

Sparkling
  • 30g coffee / 85g water (AeroPress concentrate)
  • Tonic water
  • Orange or clementine peel
  • Ice

Brew a super-concentrated AeroPress shot. Pour over ice, top with tonic, garnish with citrus peel. Clean, bright, and approachable.

Add a splash of gin if it's been one of those days.

Pink Grapefruit Cold Brew Soda

Sparkling
  • Cold brew concentrate (Ethiopian blend preferred)
  • House grapefruit syrup
  • Topo Chico or sparkling water
  • Ice

Combine cold brew concentrate with grapefruit syrup over ice. Top with Topo Chico. Ratio to taste — start 1:1:2.

Works beautifully with a Pink Lady or bright Ethiopian natural.

Cold Brew Spritz

Sparkling
  • Cold brew reduced as a syrup
  • Sparkling water
  • Fresh citrus (lemon, orange, or grapefruit)
  • Ice

Reduce cold brew to a thick syrup concentrate. Use it as you would simple syrup — build over ice with sparkling water and citrus. A clever way to showcase cold brew as an ingredient.

Dark 'n Stormy

Sparkling
  • Iced coffee or cold brew
  • Ginger beer
  • Fresh lime juice
  • Ice

Fill glass with ice. Add coffee, squeeze of lime, and float ginger beer on top. Stir gently. Coffee plays the role of dark rum.

Lemon-Ginger Cold Brew

Sparkling
  • Cold brew concentrate
  • Fresh lemon juice
  • Ginger syrup or fresh ginger
  • Sparkling or still water
  • Ice

Combine cold brew with lemon and ginger over ice. Dilute with water to taste. Bright, clean, and refreshing.

Shaken

Shakerato

Shaken
  • Double espresso
  • Simple syrup (to taste)
  • Ice (for shaking)

Pull espresso directly into a shaker tin packed with ice. Add syrup, seal, and shake hard for 10–15 seconds. Strain into a chilled coupe or rocks glass. The result is a silky, foam-topped iced espresso.

Blueberry Brown Sugar Shakerato

Shaken
  • Double espresso
  • Blueberry syrup
  • Brown sugar simple syrup
  • Ice (for shaking)

Shake espresso over ice with both syrups until frothy. Strain into a glass. The brown sugar rounds out the tartness of the blueberry against the espresso.

Espresso Coquet

Shaken
  • Double espresso
  • Coconut syrup or cream of coconut
  • Vanilla syrup
  • Fresh citrus juice
  • Pinch of cinnamon
  • Ice

Combine all ingredients in a shaker with ice. Shake until cold and frothy. Strain over fresh ice. Dust cinnamon on top.

Cold Brew

Cold Brew, Neat

Cold Brew
  • Cold brew concentrate
  • Still or sparkling water
  • Ice

Serve cold brew over ice, diluted 1:1 with water. Let the coffee speak. Best with a single-origin or blend worth showing off on its own.

Cold Brew + Coconut Water

Cold Brew
  • Cold brew concentrate
  • Coconut water
  • Ice

Sub coconut water for still water as the diluent. Naturally sweet and hydrating — great for a lighter, lower-caffeine pour.

Espresso

Black Honey Americano

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Hot water
  • Black honey (or dark raw honey)

Dissolve honey in hot water before pulling espresso over. The bitterness of the espresso balances the dark floral sweetness of black honey.

Old Fashioned Espresso

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Orange bitters
  • Walnut bitters
  • Simple syrup
  • Large ice cube

Stir espresso with both bitters and simple syrup over ice. Serve over a large cube with an expressed orange peel. Coffee as a cocktail — zero alcohol required.

Salted Maple Latte

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Maple syrup
  • Flaky sea salt
  • Steamed milk

Add maple syrup to the cup, pull espresso, steam milk and pour. Finish with a pinch of flaky salt. The salt cuts the sweetness and lifts the espresso.

Hot Honey Latte

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Hot honey
  • Steamed milk

Stir hot honey into espresso, then add steamed milk. The heat from the milk blooms the chili notes in the honey against the coffee.

Maple Chai Latte

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Chai concentrate
  • Maple syrup
  • Steamed milk

Combine espresso and chai concentrate in equal parts, sweeten with maple, and add steamed milk. Warming, spiced, and a natural menu addition for fall and winter.

Orange Zest Espresso

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Milk + splash of cream
  • Vanilla syrup
  • Fresh orange zest
  • Ground cinnamon
  • Ice

Build over ice: espresso, vanilla, milk and cream. Microplane orange zest over the top and finish with a dusting of cinnamon.

Chocolate Cherry Cortado

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Equal parts steamed milk
  • Luxardo cherry
  • Chocolate bitters

Pull espresso, add a dash of chocolate bitters, pour cortado-style with steamed milk. Drop a Luxardo cherry in the cup. Dessert in a small glass.

Mocha with Chocolate Bar

Espresso
  • Double espresso
  • Good quality chocolate bar (melted or grated)
  • Steamed milk

Melt or grate real chocolate directly into the cup rather than using sauce. Espresso pulls over the melted chocolate, then add steamed milk. The quality of the chocolate matters — use something you'd eat on its own.

Spirit-Forward

Mexican Coke Espresso

Spirit-Forward
  • Double espresso
  • Mexican Coke (cane sugar)
  • Peychaud's bitters
  • Ice

Pull espresso over ice. Add a few dashes of Peychaud's, then pour Mexican Coke slowly over the back of a spoon to layer. The bitters bridge the coffee and cola.

Mint Julep Iced Coffee

Spirit-Forward
  • Strong iced coffee or cold brew
  • Vanilla mint syrup
  • Cream
  • Crushed ice

Build over crushed ice with cold coffee, vanilla mint syrup, and a pour of cream. Serve julep-style with a mint sprig.

The Depraved: same drink, sweetened with Maker's Mark instead of syrup.

House Syrups & Components

Root Beer Simple Syrup

Syrup
  • Sassafras or sarsaparilla bark
  • Warm spices (clove, allspice, star anise)
  • Sugar
  • Water

Simmer spices in water 15 min. Strain, add equal weight sugar, stir to dissolve. Excellent in iced coffee drinks or as a mixer with sparkling water and cold brew.

Toasted Pecan Simple Syrup

Syrup
  • Raw pecans, toasted
  • Sugar
  • Water
  • Pinch of salt

Toast pecans until fragrant. Steep in hot simple syrup 30 min, then strain. Nutty, rich — pairs with espresso drinks, cold brew, or fall lattes.

Pine Simple Syrup

Syrup
  • Fresh pine needles (young tips preferred)
  • Sugar
  • Water

Rinse needles well. Steep in hot 1:1 simple syrup off heat for 20 min. Strain. Resinous and herbal — use sparingly. A few drops transforms a cold brew or americano.

Use only pesticide-free pine. Young spring tips have the mildest flavor.

Grapefruit Syrup

Syrup
  • Fresh grapefruit juice and zest
  • Sugar
  • Water

Combine juice, zest, and equal-weight sugar. Warm gently until sugar dissolves — do not boil. Strain. Bright and tart; the base for the Pink Grapefruit Cold Brew Soda.

Method Notes

Mini AeroPress Jams

Method
  • 10g coffee
  • 160g water (off boil)

A small, quick AeroPress brew — 10g coffee to 160g water. Makes a gentle, full-flavored cup without the intensity of an espresso. A great "booster" format for sampling single origins at events or on bar.

AeroPress Concentrate (Tonic Base)

Method
  • 30g coffee
  • 85g water (off boil)

Brew a high-ratio AeroPress concentrate. Use as a stand-in for espresso in iced drinks — especially tonic builds. Lower acidity and different extraction character than espresso makes it distinctly its own thing.

Coffee Soft Serve

Method
  • Cold brew concentrate
  • Whole milk or cream
  • Sugar

Blend cold brew concentrate into a custard base and churn or freeze-stir into soft serve. Serve in cups or cones. A high-impact menu item for cafes with the equipment — simple ingredients, memorable execution.
