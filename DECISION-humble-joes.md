# Humble Joe's — What It Means For Me
### Money, Time, and Room to Create

*This is my own analysis — not the pitch I hand Justin. It answers one question: if I partner with Humble Joe's, what do I get?*

---

## The Structure in One Line

**I keep Smalley. Justin becomes my production shop.** He roasts, packages, and ships. I keep the brand, the accounts, the customers, and every bit of future upside. I pay him a per-pound fee to make the coffee go away as a chore.

This is *not* a sale. I still own the business.

---

## The Money

**What I keep:** All of it — the revenue, the margin, the equity, the brand. I'm not selling anything.

**What changes:** Today I pay ~$22K/year to roast (Proper) *and* I do all the packaging and fulfillment myself for free. Under Humble Joe's, I'd pay Justin a higher per-pound fee that bundles **roast + package + ship** into one number. That fee is higher than $22K because he's doing more — but I'm buying back my time with the difference.

**The trade in plain terms:**
- I give up some margin per bag (Justin's fee > current roasting cost)
- I keep 100% ownership and 100% of the growth upside
- If Smalley is worth $80K in revenue today and I grow it to $150K, **that growth is entirely mine** — Justin's fee just scales with volume

**Why this is the "wealth" play:** I still own an asset. If I ever want to sell later — to Proper, to anyone — I sell a *bigger, cleaner* business (one that runs without me touching coffee). I'm building equity, not cashing out.

*To finalize the exact dollars, I need Justin's per-pound roast+pack+ship quote. That single number tells me my new margin.*

---

## The Time

**This is the whole point.** Here's what leaves my plate:

- Packaging **~114 five-pound bags/month** by hand
- Grinding and filling **~120 frac packs/month**
- Packing **~40 retail + ~30 DTC bags/month**
- Labeling, sealing, sorting, boxing, shipping
- Green coffee ordering and inventory juggling
- Roast-date tracking across five fill sizes

**Rough estimate: 30–50+ hours a month of physical, repetitive work — gone.**

**What stays on my plate:** Customer relationships, wholesale account management, billing, and — the part I actually want — **brand, marketing, product, and creative.** I still run the business. I just stop being the warehouse.

---

## Room to Create

This path hands me back the single scarcest thing I have — hours — and points them exactly where I want them: **building the brand I own.** New blends, storytelling, the subscription experience, marketing. I get to be the creative owner instead of the guy sealing bags at night.

---

## What I Give Up

- **Margin per bag** — Justin's fee costs more than my current roasting-only spend
- **Control of production quality** — I'm trusting his roast and his consistency
- **A dependency** — if Justin can't scale to ~650 lbs/month + five fill sizes + a grind step, this caps out fast *(the #1 thing to verify at the roastery)*

---

## Bottom Line

**Humble Joe's = keep the baby, offload the chores.** I trade a slice of margin to get 30–50 hours a month back and keep every ounce of ownership and upside. Best path if I believe in Smalley long-term and want to stay its owner and creative force — not an employee of it.

The risk is entirely about *capacity*: can Justin actually take on my volume? Everything else works.
