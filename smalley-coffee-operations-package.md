## 1. Current State of Operations

### The Business Today

Smalley Coffee is a specialty coffee brand based in Jasper, Indiana, operating with a lean, brand-forward model. The production infrastructure has been deliberately separated from the brand — the roaster was sold to create flexibility and allow the business to grow through the right partnerships rather than through fixed overhead.

**Sales Channels**
- **Direct-to-Consumer (DTC):** Shopify storefront selling 10oz bags with an existing subscription setup, customer order history, and digital infrastructure already in place
- **Wholesale:** Active accounts across Jasper, Evansville, and surrounding southern Indiana — restaurants, offices, and local retailers managed through in-person relationships and Square

**Product Lineup**

| Slow Life | The unhurried weekend cup |

**Current Production Arrangement**

**What's Working**
- Warm wholesale accounts with genuine trust in the brand

- Scalability is capped without a dedicated operational partner
- Wholesale development and subscription growth require focused, consistent attention that the current model doesn't allow

## 2. Josh's Role — Defined

The goal of any partnership structure is to build a business where each side operates in their highest-leverage lane.

**Josh's lane is growth.**

That means:
- Developing new wholesale accounts (prospecting, outreach, onboarding, relationship management)
- Managing the subscription channel end-to-end (digital ops, merchandising, customer communication, fulfillment coordination)
- Brand stewardship (voice, storytelling, product development, marketing)
- Partnership and channel development
- E-commerce strategy and execution

**What Josh is not:**
- The production operator
- The day-to-day roasting or fulfillment coordinator
- The person holding up growth because he's also managing logistics

The business stays small when one person tries to do everything. The right structure frees Josh to focus entirely on acquiring customers, building relationships, and expanding the brand — while a trusted operations partner handles roasting, packaging, and fulfillment with excellence.

## 3. The Operational Model

Regardless of which partnership path is chosen, the operational workflow follows the same logic — modeled on a structure proven to work:

```
[A0]  Smalley orders green beans + packaging → ships to roasting partner
[A1]  Customer order received via Shopify (DTC) or Square (wholesale)
  [A1.1]  Smalley handles customer service, billing, and account relationships
  [A1.2]  Roasting partner roasts, packages, and ships
[A2]  Shared inventory tracking system (Shopify app or agreed-upon tool)
[A3]  Roasting partner invoices Smalley on agreed production schedule
```

**Wholesale Orders**
Until a more automated system is in place, wholesale orders can be placed directly through Shopify using appropriate shipping addresses — keeping everything in one system and giving the roasting partner a single order queue to manage.

**The Digital Foundation**
Smalley's Shopify infrastructure is already built. The subscription setup, customer data, and order history are live. Any partner inherits a working system, not a project.

## 5. Partnership Scenario B — Proper Coffee Roasters

### The History
This isn't a new idea. Josh first approached Parlor/Proper in April 2022 with a formal proposal to build a digital subscription service on their behalf. At that time, he outlined four possible structures — from a contracted build-out to a full acquisition of Smalley — and the core logic has held ever since: Proper has the roasting depth and the cafe brand; Josh has the digital infrastructure, the growth instincts, and the operator mindset to build a channel Proper doesn't have.

The conversation has evolved. Proper is now already Smalley's roasting partner. The question isn't whether there's a fit — it's whether both sides are ready to formalize it.

### Josh's Background (Relevant Here)
Before running a cafe and restaurant, Josh's career was in digital strategy and website development and management. He built the Smalley DTC storefront from scratch, stood up the subscription infrastructure, and has managed the full customer lifecycle — acquisition, fulfillment coordination, retention, and account management. This is not a role he's growing into. It's what he's already been doing.

### The Proposal
Smalley's brand identity, wholesale accounts, and digital infrastructure absorb into Proper — with Josh joining as the operator responsible for two focused areas:

**1. Wholesale Development**
Prospecting, outreach, and onboarding of new café, restaurant, and office accounts across Jasper, Evansville, and surrounding markets. Existing Smalley accounts transition with continuity — same coffee, same relationship, same face. Proper gains immediate entry into a warm regional market it doesn't currently reach.

**2. Subscription Channel Management**
Day-to-day operations of a Proper subscription product built on the Smalley lineup. Product curation, digital merchandising, customer communication, fulfillment coordination, and subscriber growth — all managed by the person who built the system in the first place.

**What Proper Gains**
- Immediate regional wholesale presence in southern Indiana
- A ready-to-launch subscription product line with built-in brand personality
- Smalley's Shopify infrastructure (already built and operational)
- A dedicated growth operator with no onboarding curve — and a four-year track record of doing exactly this work

**What Josh Gains**
- A clear, defined role with two specific lanes
- The backing of an established roasting operation and a recognized cafe brand
- The ability to focus entirely on growth without production responsibilities

**The Subscription Case**
A subscription channel is the highest-leverage next step for any specialty roaster. Subscriptions compound — each new subscriber adds recurring revenue without recurring acquisition cost. The Smalley lineup maps directly to this model. Nine names, nine stories, nine reasons to stay subscribed.

The 2022 proposal floated this same idea. Four years later, the infrastructure is built, the accounts are real, and the ask is simpler: stop leaving the channel on the table and put someone in place to run it.

## 7. What Stays True Regardless of Path

Both scenarios work because the underlying logic is the same:

- **Josh focuses on growth** — wholesale development, subscription management, brand, marketing, and customer acquisition
- **A production partner handles operations** — roasting, packaging, fulfillment, quality control
- **The Smalley brand and product lineup are the asset** — nine distinctive names, a functioning digital storefront, and warm wholesale relationships in a market that took years to build

The risk in doing nothing is not failure. It's staying small because too much of the operation depends on one person trying to wear every hat.

The right partnership — whichever form it takes — solves that.
