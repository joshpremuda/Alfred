# The "Buy 50% of Smalley" Option
### Sell half, keep half, partner up

*A middle path between "I keep 100% and pay a roasting fee" and "I sell the whole thing." The partner buys half of Smalley, we co-own it, they handle production, I run growth.*

---

## Why This Quietly Solves the $4/lb Problem

The whole reason $4/lb hurts is that it's an **arm's-length fee** — the roaster marks up production to make *their* profit off *my* volume. My margin and their margin are in tension.

**Co-ownership erases that tension.** If my partner owns half of Smalley, they don't need to profit on the roasting fee — production runs **at cost**, and they make their money from their *ownership share of the whole business*. Suddenly the thin wholesale margin isn't being squeezed by a markup; it's just the cost of goods, and we both share what's left. The fee fight disappears.

---

## What I Get

- **Cash now** — a real check for the half I sell
- **Keep half the upside** — I still own 50%, so if we grow it, I win on both my equity *and* my role
- **Production off my plate** — my partner runs roasting/packaging/fulfillment
- **Stay the creative owner** — I keep running brand, sales, and growth on a business I still half-own
- **A committed partner** — they own it too, so they're motivated to grow, not just bill me

This hits all three of my goals at once: **money now, time back, and I still get to build the brand.**

---

## What It's Worth (rough)

Smalley does ~$80K revenue, but on thin margins — so value comes from the **brand, accounts, and growth potential**, not current earnings. A fair full-business range is **~$45–60K**. That puts **50% at roughly $22–30K.**

*(Thin margins keep the multiple modest — but the brand, the 9-blend catalog, the wholesale book, and the working Shopify/subscription system are real, hard-to-replicate assets. That's the argument for the top of the range.)*

---

## The Catches (worth naming honestly)

1. **It requires a valuation conversation.** Justin's email said he wanted to avoid pricing — a buy-in forces a number. Not bad, just real.
2. **It requires the partner to have capital.** $22–30K cash is easy for Proper; it may be a stretch for Justin (a small roaster). If Justin, it might have to be paid over time or from profit distributions.
3. **50/50 can deadlock.** Two owners with equal say and no tiebreaker is a classic trap. **I should keep 51% (control) or we define upfront who decides what** — especially brand/creative, which is my lane.

---

## Who This Fits Better

**Proper — strong fit.** They have the capital, they already roast, and buying half (instead of all) lets me **keep 50% of the upside** rather than fully cashing out. This is a great "in between" versus their full-acquisition proposal — I get cash *and* keep a stake in something I keep growing. If I like Proper's stability but don't want to hand over the whole thing, **buy-50 is the sweet spot.**

**Justin — possible, but capital-dependent.** The co-ownership beautifully solves the fee problem and matches his "build together" language. But it hinges on whether he can fund a buy-in. If he can't pay cash, this drifts back toward the JV (where he contributes production *as* his stake instead of buying in). Worth floating to see if he has appetite and capital.

---

## How I'd Frame It

- **To Proper:** "Instead of buying all of Smalley, what if you bought half? You get the accounts, the brand, and me building your e-commerce — and I stay a motivated co-owner growing it with you."
- **To Justin:** "What if instead of me paying you per pound, you bought into Smalley? You'd own half, production runs at cost, and we both grow the same pie — which is closer to what we both said we wanted."

---

## Bottom Line

Buy-50 is the cleanest structure I've found for hitting all three goals — **cash now, time back, keep creating** — without fully giving up the brand. It solves the $4/lb tension by aligning incentives instead of negotiating a rate. It fits **Proper best** (they have the capital), and is worth floating to **Justin** to see if he has the appetite to buy in rather than charge a fee.

The two things to protect: **a fair valuation** (top of range, given the assets) and **51% / defined control** so I keep the wheel on the brand I built.
