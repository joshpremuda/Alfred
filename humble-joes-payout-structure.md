# Humble Joe's — How the Payout Works
### Designing a Partnership, Not a Vendor Deal

*Justin's email is clear: he wants a real partnership — "growth engine / production engine," "who owns what," "sustainable for years." That's not a per-pound roasting fee. It's a shared business. Here's how people actually get paid in that structure.*

---

## The Big Shift

**Vendor model (what we're moving away from):** I own Smalley 100%, Justin roasts for a fee. Simple, but it makes Justin a contractor — exactly what his email says he *doesn't* want.

**Partnership model (what he's asking for):** We build a shared business where each of us gets paid from the same pie, in proportion to what we own and what we do. No lump sum, no buyout — ongoing income that grows as the business grows.

In a partnership, "payout" isn't one check. It's **three distinct ways money flows to me**, and we design each one.

---

## The Three Ways I Get Paid

### 1. A brand license fee — off the top, before anything is split
I own the Smalley brand, the accounts, the Shopify system, and the ~$80K book of business. **I should not just fold that in for free.** The cleanest way to protect it: I *license* the brand to the partnership rather than hand it over. The partnership pays me a royalty (e.g., ~5% of revenue) before profit is calculated.

- Protects the value I built — if this ever unwinds, **I keep my brand**
- Rewards me for the asset I bring, separate from the work I do
- ~$4K/year at today's revenue, growing with sales

### 2. Profit distributions — my share of what's left
After costs (green, packaging, shipping) and the brand royalty, the operating profit gets split by ownership. Since our strengths are genuinely 50/50 in spirit, that's the natural anchor — **but** I'm bringing an existing, revenue-generating business and he's bringing capacity that starts by serving *my* accounts. So the split should credit that:
- Option A: Start weighted in my favor (e.g., 60/40), moving toward 50/50 as Justin's production investment proves out
- Option B: Straight 50/50, with the brand royalty (above) as my compensation for bringing the existing business

### 3. A draw for the work I do — optional
If we both take a market-rate draw for our roles (me: sales/brand; him: production), that comes out before profit split. Or we skip draws and just split profit. This is a lifestyle/cash-flow choice we each make.

---

## The Payout Waterfall (how a dollar moves)

```
Revenue (~$80K today, growing)
  − Cost of goods (green coffee, packaging, shipping)
  − Brand license royalty to me (~5%)        ← I get paid here (asset)
  − Role draws, if we take them               ← I get paid here (labor), optional
  = Operating profit
  − Split by ownership %                       ← I get paid here (equity)
```

Three touchpoints. The beauty: as the business grows, **all three grow** — and Justin is now motivated to grow it with me, not just collect a roasting fee.

---

## The Three Decisions That Set Everything

Justin's right that structure comes before price. These three decisions *are* the deal:

1. **Who owns the brand/IP?**
   My strong recommendation: **I keep it and license it in.** Contributing it outright means I could lose my own brand if the partnership fails. Licensing keeps my downside protected while still fully committing to the partnership.

2. **Who owns the partnership entity, and in what split?**
   Anchor at 50/50 (matches his "complement each other" framing), adjusted for the existing business I bring — via either a weighted split or the brand royalty.

3. **Do we take draws, or just split profit?**
   Depends on how much cash each of us needs monthly vs. reinvesting in growth.

---

## How This Serves My Goals

- **Money:** I keep ownership *and* future upside — this is the wealth play. I get paid three ways, all of which grow. No cashing out.
- **Time:** Production and packaging (~30–50 hrs/month) move to Justin — the whole reason to do this.
- **Create:** I become the growth-and-brand half of a two-person business, with a committed partner handling everything physical. Exactly the role I want.

## What I'd Say Back to Justin

He asked to design the partnership before talking price — that's the right instinct and I should meet him there. My reply frames the *structure*:
- Yes to partnership, not a vendor deal
- I bring brand + accounts + digital + growth; he brings production + systems + fulfillment
- Let's define ownership (I'd keep/license the brand), how profit is shared, and what each of us owns operationally
- The dollars follow the structure — agreed

*Don't lead with numbers. Lead with "here's how I think we each get paid, fairly, forever."*
