# Draft Note to Justin — On the $4/lb

Justin,

First — thank you for putting a real number on the table. That helps, and I appreciate you making it concrete.

I've been sitting with the $4/lb, and I want to be honest with you about where I land, because I think honesty now saves us both headaches later.

**The part I struggle with is the math on the wholesale side.** Most of my volume is the 5lb bags. Once I add up green coffee, your fee, shipping, and the platform cuts, there's very little margin left on those bags — to the point where growing my wholesale business would barely move the needle for me. I don't think a flat per-pound fee is *wrong*; it just runs into the reality that my biggest product is also my thinnest, and $4 across the board leaves almost nothing to build on.

The other thing I keep coming back to: a flat fee makes you my roaster, not my partner — and everything you said in your last note pointed the other way. You talked about building something together, each of us doing what we're wired for, and growing it for years. A per-pound invoice doesn't really capture that.

**So here's where my head is at instead.** Rather than me paying you a fixed fee per bag, what if we built this as a real partnership — where you own a piece of the growth alongside me? You run production, I run sales and brand, and we both win as it grows, instead of you earning the same $4 whether we sell 100 bags or 1,000. That way you're not just covering your costs on my volume — you're building equity in something we grow together. It aligns us, and honestly it's closer to what we both said we wanted when we sat down.

If a full partnership feels like too much too fast, a simpler middle step could work too — something like a lower rate on the easy-to-pack bulk bags and the $4 on the smaller retail bags, since those take more of your hands. But I'd rather aim at the bigger idea if you're open to it.

None of this changes how much I enjoyed the visit or how much I want to find a way to do this together. I just don't want to start us down a road where the numbers quietly work against one of us.

Let's keep talking.

Josh
