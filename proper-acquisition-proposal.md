# A Proposal for Proper Coffee to Acquire Smalley Coffee
**Presented by Josh Premuda | Smalley Coffee | Jasper, Indiana**
**July 2026 | Confidential**

---

## The Opportunity in One Paragraph

Proper already roasts Smalley's coffee — roughly $22,000 in roasting business last year alone. Smalley will do an estimated $80,000 in revenue this year across wholesale and e-commerce. This proposal is for Proper to acquire Smalley Coffee — the accounts, the coffee line, the Shopify infrastructure, and the customer base — and fold it fully into Proper. The coffees keep their names and their stories; they simply become Proper coffees. Dawn Patrol from Smalley Coffee becomes **Dawn Patrol from Proper Coffee**. Josh is retained long-term to run the line and build out e-commerce ordering across all of Proper. One brand, a deeper catalog, a new digital channel, and an operator who's spent four years building all of it.

---

## What Proper Is Acquiring

**1. Revenue that Proper already roasts for**
Smalley is on track for ~$80,000 in revenue this year. Proper produced this coffee — it knows the volume, the cadence, and the product firsthand. There is no diligence mystery here. The $22,000 Smalley spent with Proper last year is the floor of what this relationship is worth; the acquisition captures the whole margin stack, not just the roasting fee.

**2. Wholesale accounts in a market Proper doesn't reach**
Active, warm accounts across Jasper, Evansville, and the southern Indiana corridor — restaurants, offices, and retailers with established buying patterns and trust in the brand. Built through years of in-person relationships. These transition with continuity: same coffee, same face, same delivery rhythm.

**3. A subscription-ready coffee line that becomes Proper's**
Nine named blends — Dawn Patrol, Slow Life, Soul Sessions, Game Face, Farm Hand, Power Broker, Night Moves, Groundswell, Motoring Oil — each with a distinct identity and customer. Under this proposal, the names and stories are kept but the Smalley label is retired: these become Proper coffees, full stop. Proper doesn't inherit a second brand to maintain — it inherits a finished, characterful catalog under its own name. This is a ready-made subscription lineup, not a naming project and not a co-branding arrangement.

**4. Working digital infrastructure**
A live Shopify storefront with subscription billing, customer data, and order history. Proper inherits a functioning e-commerce operation on day one — and a platform that can be extended to carry Proper's own retail bags, wholesale ordering, and subscriptions.

**5. The operator**
Josh's background before coffee was digital strategy and website development/management. He built Smalley's storefront, subscription system, and wholesale book from scratch. The acquisition includes the person who knows how every piece works.

## The Deal Structure

Two components, priced separately: the **acquisition** (what Proper pays for the business) and the **role** (what Proper pays for Josh's ongoing work). Keeping these separate keeps the deal clean for both sides.

### Component 1 — Acquiring Smalley

Three ways to structure it. Josh is open to any of these or a negotiated blend:

**Option A — Lump Sum**
A one-time payment of **$45,000–$55,000** at closing.
This reflects roughly 0.6x of current-year revenue — conservative for a business with recurring wholesale accounts, a subscription base, and built infrastructure, and modest against what it would cost Proper to build this market presence and digital channel from zero. Clean, simple, done.

**Option B — Royalty (No Cash Upfront)**
**8% of acquired-line revenue for 5 years**, paid quarterly.
At current revenue this is ~$6,400/year; if the brand grows under Proper as expected, the royalty grows with it. Proper takes on no upfront risk — it only pays out of revenue the brand actually generates. Total payout scales with success, with a cap negotiable (e.g., $60,000 lifetime cap).

**Option C — Hybrid (Recommended)**
**$20,000 at closing + 5% of acquired-line revenue for 4 years**, paid quarterly.
This is the structure Josh believes is fairest to both sides: Proper's upfront exposure is less than one year of its existing roasting revenue from Smalley, and the royalty aligns both parties around growing the brand rather than arguing about a static price. At flat revenue, total consideration lands around $36,000; if the brand doubles, both sides win.

*In all options: "acquired-line revenue" means revenue from the nine transitioned coffees and the transitioned wholesale accounts — even after they carry the Proper name — tracked through the Shopify/Square records. Clean to measure, hard to dispute.*

### Component 2 — Retaining Josh

Josh joins Proper long-term in a defined role: **Packaged Coffee & E-Commerce Lead, Proper Coffee**.

**The role covers two lanes:**

*Running the packaged coffee line*
- Managing and growing the transitioned wholesale accounts
- Prospecting new café, restaurant, and office accounts in the southern Indiana corridor
- Operating the subscription channel end-to-end: lineup, merchandising, customer communication, retention, growth
- Stewarding the nine coffees' names and stories through the transition to the Proper label

*Building e-commerce for Proper*
- Extending the existing Shopify infrastructure into a full ordering platform for Proper: retail bags, subscriptions, and streamlined wholesale ordering
- Owning digital merchandising, the customer data pipeline, and the online customer experience across both brands
- Building the systems so online revenue runs without daily hand-holding

**Compensation structure (open to negotiation):**
- A base retainer or salary appropriate to a part-time-to-start, growth-oriented role
- Commission on new wholesale accounts Josh opens
- A percentage of e-commerce/subscription revenue growth above an agreed baseline
- Reviewed annually against agreed growth targets

The principle: a modest fixed component, with real upside tied to the growth Josh actually delivers. Proper shouldn't pay for promises — it should pay for results, and Josh is comfortable betting on his own execution.

## Why This Works for Proper

- **Margin capture on revenue it already produces.** Proper roasted ~$22,000 of this coffee last year and kept only the roasting fee. Post-acquisition, it keeps the wholesale and retail margin too.
- **A southern Indiana wholesale footprint, instantly.** Warm accounts that would take years and real salary dollars to build cold.
- **An e-commerce platform without a build project.** The Shopify system exists, works, and comes with the person who built it.
- **A subscription product that's launch-ready — under Proper's own name.** Nine blends with identity and an existing subscriber base, no second brand to maintain. The highest-leverage recurring-revenue channel in specialty coffee.
- **De-risked by history.** This isn't an acquisition of a stranger. Proper knows the product, the volumes, and Josh. The 2022 conversation planted this idea; four years of working relationship have proven it out.

## Why This Works for Josh

- Fair value for a business built over years — paid through a structure that respects Proper's cash position
- A long-term role doing exactly what he's best at: product, accounts, and digital growth
- Production and fulfillment permanently off his plate, with the coffees he created living on — same names, same stories — under a roaster he trusts

## Proposed Path Forward

1. **Conversation** — walk through this proposal, answer questions, pick a preferred structure
2. **Numbers review** — Josh opens the books: Shopify/Square revenue records, account list, subscription metrics
3. **Terms sheet** — one page covering acquisition structure, royalty definition, and role/compensation
4. **Transition plan** — a 60–90 day handover: accounts introduced, Shopify migrated under Proper, packaging relabeled to the Proper name, fulfillment workflow formalized
5. **Go** — Josh starts in-role; first-year targets set for wholesale growth and e-commerce revenue
