# Justin — A Multiyear Buy-In to Smalley
### How Justin becomes a 50% owner over time, without needing cash up front

*The reframe: instead of me paying Justin a per-pound fee, Justin buys into Smalley gradually and we co-own it. Production runs at cost, we split the profit, and we grow the same pie. This is the "build something together" he asked for — made affordable.*

---

## The Core Idea

Justin doesn't have $25K sitting in a drawer — and he doesn't need it. He buys his stake **over several years**, funded largely by the very production margin he was going to charge me anyway.

**The key move:**
- His $4/lb offer had his *profit* built into it.
- Instead of me paying that profit as a fee, **production runs at cost**, and the margin Justin would've earned **accrues toward his purchase of 50% of Smalley.**
- Same money for Justin — but now he's building ownership instead of sending invoices, and my wholesale margin stops getting squeezed.

---

## The Numbers (illustrative)

- **Full value of Smalley:** ~$50K (midpoint of a $45–60K range — value is in the brand, accounts, and Shopify system, not thin current earnings)
- **50% stake:** ~$25,000
- **Buy-in period:** 4–5 years
- **Annual buy-in:** ~$5,000–6,250/year (~$420–520/month equivalent)

That's a very manageable number — and much of it can come from production value and profit share rather than out-of-pocket cash.

---

## How Justin Funds the Buy-In (three sources, blendable)

1. **Foregone production margin** — production runs at cost; the markup he would've charged instead counts toward his stake
2. **Applied profit share** — his half of the business's profit distributions gets applied to the purchase until it's paid off
3. **Modest cash** — optional, to fill any gap

The more it's funded by 1 and 2, the less real cash Justin needs — which is the whole point.

---

## How I Stay Protected

1. **Equity vests as he pays.** Justin's ownership grows as the buy-in is funded (e.g., 10%/year over 5 years). If he stops or walks, he keeps only the vested portion — I don't hand over half on a promise.
2. **I keep control during (and ideally after) the buy-in.** I hold **51%** until fully bought in, and I keep final say on brand and creative — that's my lane and my name.
3. **A written note.** The buy-in is documented — amount, schedule, vesting, what happens if he stops. Simple, but real.
4. **The brand question.** Decide up front: does the Smalley brand/IP sell into the co-owned company, or do I keep it personally and license it in? Licensing keeps my ultimate safety net — worth considering.

---

## Why This Works For Everyone

**For me:**
- **Income over time** — paid for half the business across several years (fine, since I don't need a lump sum)
- **Keep 50% + control** — I still own half the upside and steer the brand
- **Production off my plate** — Justin runs roasting/packaging/fulfillment
- **Protected margin** — production at cost, no fee squeeze
- **A truly committed partner** — he's an owner, not a vendor

**For Justin:**
- **Real ownership** with no big check required
- **The "build together for years" he explicitly wants**
- **His trailer + Humble Joe's brand stay 100% his**
- **He earns his way in** through the work he's already doing

---

## Open Decisions Before I Pitch It

1. **Valuation** — land on the full-business number (I'd anchor ~$50–55K given the assets)
2. **Buy-in length** — 4 or 5 years
3. **Funding mix** — how much from foregone margin vs. profit share vs. cash
4. **Control** — confirm I hold 51% and brand authority
5. **Brand IP** — sell it in, or keep and license
6. **Governance** — who decides what; how we handle a disagreement or an exit

---

## Bottom Line

A multiyear buy-in turns Justin's rejected $4/lb fee into the fuel for co-ownership. He gets the partnership he asked for without needing capital; I get paid over time, keep half the upside and control, and offload production without my margin getting squeezed. It's the cleanest answer yet to "how do we build this together."
