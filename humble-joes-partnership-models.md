# Humble Joe's — How a Partnership Could Look
### Four concrete shapes, from lightest to deepest

*Justin wants to "build something together" and own it "for years." That points past a vendor deal. Here are the real structures, what each means for ownership, money, and my time — and which one I'd pick.*

---

## First, what each of us brings

| | Josh / Smalley | Justin / Humble Joe's |
|---|---|---|
| **Production** | ~200 lbs/wk of demand | ~320 lbs/wk + roaster + facility |
| **Brand** | Smalley name, 9 blends, stories | Humble Joe's name, trailer |
| **Market** | Wholesale accounts, DTC, ~$80K book | Events/weddings/corporate via trailer |
| **Skills** | Sales, brand, digital, growth | Roasting, production, systems, quality |
| **Role wanted** | Growth engine | Production engine |

Combined production is ~520 lbs/wk — I bring ~38% of it, plus the brand and the book. **I'm a near-equal, not a small add-on.** That matters for the split.

---

## Model 1 — Production Alliance (lightest)

**What it is:** We stay two separate businesses. Justin roasts, packs, and ships Smalley under a partnership agreement — better than a vendor deal (preferred pricing, shared growth targets, a profit-share kicker), but no shared company.

- **Ownership:** Unchanged. I keep 100% of Smalley.
- **Money — me:** I keep all Smalley profit; pay Justin production cost + a growth bonus.
- **Money — Justin:** Production revenue + a small share of Smalley's growth.
- **Pros:** Simplest, cheapest legally, fully reversible, I keep everything.
- **Cons:** Doesn't really satisfy Justin's "own something together." Feels close to the vendor deal he said he *doesn't* want.

---

## Model 2 — Joint Venture / NewCo (recommended)

**What it is:** We form a new company that runs the packaged-coffee growth business. I license the Smalley brand into it (keeping ownership); Justin contributes roasting capacity and fulfillment. We co-own the JV and split its profit.

- **Ownership:** New LLC, equity split ~50/50 (adjusted for the book + brand I bring). **I keep the Smalley brand personally and license it in — so if this ever unwinds, I walk away with my brand.**
- **Roles:** Me = growth engine (sales, brand, digital, accounts). Justin = production engine (roast, pack, ship, quality).
- **Money — me:** Brand royalty off the top + sales commission (income now) + my equity share of profit. Optional draw.
- **Money — Justin:** Production draw/margin + his equity share of profit.
- **Two flavors:**
  - **2a (start here):** JV runs only the Smalley-branded business. Humble Joe's brand + trailer stay 100% Justin's.
  - **2b (later):** JV combines *both* brands' packaged/wholesale coffee into one operation.
- **Pros:** Real shared ownership ("build together"), shared upside, I keep my brand, income now via royalty + commission, production off my plate.
- **Cons:** Needs a real operating agreement (ownership %, roles, exit terms, what happens if someone leaves). Worth doing right.

---

## Model 3 — Full Merger (deepest)

**What it is:** Combine Smalley and Humble Joe's into one company — both brands, the roaster, the trailer, everything, under shared ownership.

- **Ownership:** One entity, we split it. I give up sole ownership of Smalley entirely.
- **Money:** Everything pooled; we each draw + share profit by ownership.
- **Pros:** Maximum scale, truly "bigger than both of us," one combined brand story.
- **Cons:** I lose independent ownership of my brand. Hardest to unwind. Commingles his trailer business with mine and ties us together completely. Big commitment for a relationship this new.

---

## My Recommendation: Model 2a

Form a joint venture for the Smalley packaged-coffee business. Here's why it fits my goals *and* Justin's:

**For me:**
- **Income now** — brand royalty + sales commission pay me from day one, not "if there's profit"
- **Keep my brand** — I license it in, I don't hand it over; protected if it falls apart
- **Time back** — Justin owns all production and fulfillment
- **Create** — I'm the growth/brand half of a real company, with a committed partner on ops
- **Upside** — I still own equity in a business I'm growing

**For Justin:**
- Real co-ownership — the "build something together" he asked for
- His trailer + brand stay 100% his (nothing to give up)
- He's motivated to grow with me, not just bill me
- A clear production-engine lane, which is what he said he wants

**Why not go straight to a merger:** the relationship is new. Start with a JV on the Smalley business, prove we work well together for a year or two, then deepen to combining both brands (2b) or a full merger if it's earning it. Structure that can grow — exactly Justin's "for years to come."

---

## The Split Logic (when he asks)

I bring ~38% of combined volume **plus** the brand, the accounts, the digital infrastructure, and the growth role. That argues for something genuinely near-equal — call it **45–55%** — which lines up with Justin's own "we complement each other" instinct. The brand royalty and my sales commission sit *outside* the equity split, so they compensate the extra value I bring without me having to fight for a lopsided ownership number.
