## Where I Am and What I'm Looking For

A few years ago I sold the roaster. Not because I wanted to step away from coffee — but because I realized where I create the most value, and it isn't behind the drum.

My background is in digital strategy, brand building, and sales. I know how to grow wholesale accounts, run an e-commerce operation, build a subscription channel, and tell a story that makes people care about what's in their cup. That's where I want to spend my time.

What I need is a production partner who is excellent at what I'm not — sourcing, roasting, quality, and fulfillment. Someone who takes as much pride in their work behind the roaster as I take in building a brand in front of it.

From our conversation, it sounds like you're in a similar place: you know where your strengths are, and you're thinking about what it looks like to build something bigger than what either of us could do alone.

That's exactly what I'm trying to figure out too.

## What a Partnership Could Look Like

This is an early conversation, and I don't want to get ahead of where we are. But here's the operational model I've been working from — it's straightforward and it's designed so both sides stay in their lane.

```
[A0]  Smalley sources and orders green beans + packaging → ships to Humble Joe's
[A1]  Order received (Shopify for DTC / Square for wholesale)
   [A1.1]  Smalley handles customer service, billing, account relationships
   [A1.2]  Humble Joe's roasts, packages, and ships
[A2]  Shared inventory tracking (Shopify app or agreed tool)
[A3]  Humble Joe's invoices Smalley per production run
```

**On the wholesale side:** I manage the accounts in person. Until we automate further, I place wholesale orders directly in Shopify with the right shipping address — one queue for you to fulfill.

**On the DTC side:** Shopify drives everything. Subscription orders, one-time purchases, and new customer acquisition all flow through the same system. The infrastructure is already built.

**What this means for you:** You roast and ship. I handle everything customer-facing. There's no overlap, no confusion about who owns what.

**A subscription-ready product line** — Nine blends with built-in personality. This lineup maps directly to a subscription model that drives recurring revenue without recurring acquisition cost.

**Digital infrastructure** — A functioning Shopify storefront with subscription setup, customer data, and order history. Already built. Not a project.

**Brand and growth focus** — Someone dedicated to acquiring new accounts, growing the subscriber base, and building the brand while you focus entirely on the coffee.

## What I'd Want to Understand About Humble Joe's

Before we talk about what this could become, I want to make sure I understand your operation clearly. Some questions I'm bringing to Friday's visit:

1. **Capacity** — What's your current roasting volume, and where's your ceiling? What does adding Smalley's production look like against your existing workload?
2. **Roast profiles** — Can we walk through the Smalley lineup and talk about how you'd approach each one?
3. **Fulfillment** — How do you currently handle packaging and shipping? What systems are you using?
4. **Timeline** — If we decided to move forward, what would onboarding look like and how quickly could we be operational?
5. **The mobile trailer** — How does that business fit alongside roasting? Is it a separate lane or does it draw from the same time and capacity?
6. **What you're looking for** — What would make this worth it for you? What does the ideal version of this relationship look like from your side?

## What Success Looks Like

Twelve months from now, the version of this that's working looks like:

- Humble Joe's is roasting and fulfilling Smalley orders with consistency and quality
- Smalley's wholesale account base has grown meaningfully in southern Indiana
- The subscription channel is generating reliable recurring revenue
- Both sides are operating entirely in their lane — you're not doing sales, I'm not doing roasting
- The relationship has earned enough trust to think about what comes next

Neither of us is trying to run the other's business. The goal is to build one together that neither could build alone.
